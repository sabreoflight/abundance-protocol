# The Abundance Protocol — Draft v0.1
### Mission Abundance · a practice for harmonizing thought, action, and deed

---

## 1. The design principle: two layers

| Layer | Purpose | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **The Practice** | Single-minded, distilled, actionable. Follows the book's directive: *focus only on getting rich.* No debate, no digressions. | The main videos: meditative, about 5 minutes, same structure every time. |
| **The Understanding** | The reasoning and philosophy the book declined to give: where each principle comes from and why it may work. Optional. | Companion videos ("Why It Works"), descriptions, and articles. |

The viewer can stay in the Practice layer forever. The Understanding layer is for anyone who wants to know why.

---

## 2. The Protocol (8 steps, one spine: **Mind → Action → Deed**)

| # | Step | The practice (one line) | Principle | Lineage and "why it may work" (Understanding layer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Choose the lens** | Decide: there is enough, and more can be created. | Abundance is a way of seeing before it is a result. | Wattles' creative-plane idea. Stoics: you control your attention, not circumstances. Emerson on the self-created world. |
| 2 | **Clarify the vision** | Write exactly what you want. Hold the completed picture. | A clear intention organizes attention and effort. | Wattles' "clear mental image". Hill's "definiteness of purpose". Vedic *sankalpa* (a deliberately held intention). Modern goal-setting research. |
| 3 | **Gratitude** | Name three things you already have. Give thanks in advance. | Gratitude is the bridge from lack to abundance. | Present in nearly every contemplative tradition. Research links gratitude practice to well-being and resilience. Wattles: gratitude keeps the mind from accepting lack as permanent. |
| 4 | **Release competition** | Let others' light be light. Desire by creating, never by taking. | Value is created, not divided. | Buddhist *mudita* (joy in others' good fortune). Emerson's "Compensation". Non-zero-sum economics. |
| 5 | **Act daily, in a Certain Way** | Make each day a success. Put conviction into each task. | Thought must become action. | Bhagavad Gita on *karma yoga* (full effort, released from anxiety over results). Habit and compounding research. |
| 6 | **Give first** | More use-value than cash-value. Leave every person better off. | Value that circulates returns. | *Dana* (generosity) in Buddhist and Hindu traditions. Reciprocity research. Wattles' "increase". |
| 7 | **Release hurry, hold faith** | Calm, unhurried attention. Act as one who is already receiving. | Hurry is fear in motion. | Taoist *wu-wei* (effortless action). Stoic equanimity. Wattles' "faith". |
| 8 | **Daily alignment** | Morning breath and affirmation. | Ritual anchors attention. | Contemplative practice across traditions. Implementation-intention research. |

## 4. Voice and guardrails

- **Practice, not promise.** The book speaks of riches flowing "with the certainty of mathematical law." That is a claim, and the channel should present these ideas as a discipline for focus and conduct, not a guaranteed outcome. This protects viewers and the channel's credibility. A short line in each description is enough, for example: *"This is a contemplative practice, not financial or professional advice."*
- **Name the lineage honestly.** Where an idea comes from Wattles, New Thought, the Stoics, the Gita, or Buddhism, say so. That makes the Understanding layer strong.
- **Hard times stay general.** Refer to "hard seasons" and don't name specific painful events. Keep the tone warm and steady.
- **Look and sound:** deep gradient backgrounds, gold for emphasized words, slow motion, breathing practice at the end of every episode, the same ambient pad and chime.
