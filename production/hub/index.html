The microsite

A complete, self-contained course site, built and tested in the cloud session. It's ready to install and deploy. The package is /home/user/mission-abundance-site.tar.gz (about 300 MB, nearly all of it the eight lesson videos). What it does
  • Sales page built from the final copy, with both trailers, the eight steps, pricing, guarantee and FAQ.
  • Stripe Checkout for the one-time $9 payment, with promotion codes enabled for gift and creator codes.
  • Instant access: the buyer sets a password on the page right after paying, so no email system is required.
  • Student accounts with sign-in, sign-out, password reset and an account page.
  • Protected lesson player: videos stream only to signed-in buyers, with no public video links.
  • Progress tracking, worksheet and tracker downloads, and audio downloads that appear automatically once recorded.
  • Webhooks grant access on payment and revoke it on refund. Duplicate events are harmless.
  • Onboarding emails on days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30, when email sending is configured.
  • Admin commands: list students, stats and revenue, gift access, revoke, and reset links.
  • Voice-over pipeline: one command mixes your recordings in, ducks the music under your voice, and exports MP3s.
  • Deploy files: a Dockerfile, docker-compose, and a .env template. It runs on Node 22 with no database server.
Tested here
  • 30 automated end-to-end checks pass, from public pages through purchase, password, lessons, streaming, downloads, progress and sign-in, to signed Stripe webhooks for payment, a duplicate event and a refund.
  • A clean install from the package passes all 30.
  • Production mode refuses to start with missing settings or a weak secret. Test checkout switches itself off, forged webhooks are rejected, and security headers are on.
  • In the browser, pages render on desktop and phone with no sideways scrolling and no console errors, and lesson video loads at full length.
  • Voice-over pipeline was tested with stand-in tones: full-length output, music ducked about 5 dB, and the MP3 exported.
  • Not tested here: a full container build. Docker Hub rate-limited this environment, and the build's package install can't reach the internet from here. Both work on a normal server.

A3. The problem

Most of us run on a quiet scarcity script.

There is only so much. If they rise, I fall. Time is running out. I'll feel abundant when I finally have more.

That script turns money, attention and even happiness into a contest. It speeds up your breath and narrows your focus. It keeps your eyes on other people's lanes instead of on what you are building.

And because it is a lens, more effort doesn't fix it. Working harder through a scarcity lens only shows you more scarcity.

A4. The shift

There is another way to see the same room.

On the creative plane, value is made, not divided. Your attention works like light: what you focus on grows. Gratitude is the bridge from feeling lack to feeling abundant, even before the facts have changed. And calm, unhurried action does more than frantic effort ever did.

The Abundance Protocol turns that shift into eight practical steps you can live, one morning at a time.

A5. The eight steps

The steps run Mind, then Action, then Deed.

  1. Choose the lens: there is enough, and more can be created.
  2. Clarify the vision: know exactly what you want and hold the completed picture.
  3. Gratitude: the bridge from lack to abundance.
  4. Release competition: step off the race and create instead.
  5. Act daily in a Certain Way: one complete day, one task, full attention.
  6. Give first: more in use-value than you take in cash-value.
  7. Release hurry and hold faith: do your part completely and trust the process.
  8. Daily alignment: a five-minute morning practice that holds all the others.

A9. Where it comes from

The Abundance Protocol is inspired by The Science of Getting Rich, written by Wallace D. Wattles in 1910. Wattles distilled his ideas to a single purpose and refused to explain the reasoning behind them.
